## The Golden Rule: Never Pay Anyone for a Job

The single most reliable barrier against employment fraud is uncompromising: **it is free to apply; never pay anyone for a job.**

Across Europe, fraudsters run two primary payment traps disguised as "remote AI data annotation" or "AI model rating" roles:

1. **Upfront Fee & Certification Traps:** A fake recruiter claims you have been pre-selected for a high-paying remote AI trainer role, provided you first purchase a mandatory "AI Prompt Engineering Certificate," pay for an external "background check," or send a refundable "security deposit" for a laptop. Legitimate platforms never charge candidates for assessments, vetting, or hardware.
2. **Account Activation & Payout Release Fees:** After asking you to complete a few sample tasks on a spoofed dashboard that displays a fake earnings balance, the scammer demands a "tax clearance fee," "Stripe activation deposit," or USDT cryptocurrency transfer before you can withdraw your money.

On Mercor, applying and interviewing cost `$0`. When you are matched to a real project (which is never guaranteed, as many applicants wait weeks or receive no project offer), you work on your own computer and are paid as an independent contractor, usually weekly via Stripe in US dollars. ## Common Telegram and WhatsApp "Task Scam" Red Flags

Both Europol and the Finnish National Cyber Security Centre (*Kyberturvallisuuskeskus*) warn against unsolicited WhatsApp, Telegram, or SMS messages claiming to recruit "remote AI reviewers." Watch for these five red flags:

### 1. Unsolicited Instant Messaging Outreach with Instant Hiring
Legitimate AI-training platforms require résumé uploads, credential checks, and structured assessments before matching you to any project. If a stranger messages you on WhatsApp or Telegram offering `$100–300/day` with zero interview, it is a scam.

### 2. "App Optimization" or "Merchant Rating" Tasks Disguised as AI Training
Real AI-training work involves writing complex domain prompts, grading model responses against rubrics, and verifying legal, medical, or software reasoning. Scammers use the phrase "AI training" to disguise fake click-farm tasks where you press buttons inside a fraudulent web app.

### 3. Cryptocurrency Top-Ups ("Combo Tasks")
In a classic Telegram task scam, the fraudster sends you €10 or €20 after a sample task to build trust, then locks your account on a "combination task" and demands €100 to €2,000 in cryptocurrency to withdraw your balance. **No legitimate AI-training platform ever asks for crypto deposits.**

### 4. Guaranteed Daily Income Promises
Any message promising guaranteed daily or weekly earnings is fraudulent. In real AI-training contracting, **work is not guaranteed; many people wait weeks or get no project**, and all posted figures (such as `$40–70/h` for Generalists or `$70–150/h` for Software Engineers on `2026-11-05`) are posted ranges, not guaranteed payouts.

### 5. Requests to Install Remote-Access Software
Never install remote desktop tools (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) or unknown executables sent via chat to "configure your AI workspace."
