## Why Remote AI-Training Work Triggers Self-Employment Rules

When specialists explore remote AI training jobs in Europe—whether through [AI-Training Work for Generalists](/ai-training-for-generalists/) (`$40–70/h` posted range on `2026-11-05`, not guaranteed) or credentialed pools in law, medicine, and software engineering—they often assume that logging a few hours from home works like a casual part-time job (*osa-aikatyö*).

In legal and social-insurance terms, the relationship is completely different. When you work on a project through Mercor, you do not sign a Finnish or European employment contract (*työsopimus*) with employer-withheld payroll taxes or paid annual leave. Instead:

1. **You work as an independent contractor:** You choose when to log hours within project guidelines, use your own hardware, and act as an independent service provider without an employer-employee relationship.
2. **You are paid in US dollars via Stripe:** Payouts are usually processed weekly in USD via Stripe. No European employer withholds preliminary tax (*ennakonpidätys*) or statutory social contributions from these payouts.
3. **Work is irregular and never guaranteed:** Applying is free, and many applicants wait weeks or receive no project offer at all. Even when assigned to a project, hours can fluctuate sharply or pause without warning when a model evaluation sprint finishes.

Because of this structure, European unemployment authorities classify remote AI-training contractor work as self-employment (*yrittäjyys* in Finland, *selbständige Tätigkeit* in Germany, *activité non salariée* in France). Treating contractor work as unreported casual wage work is the most common mistake that triggers benefit freezes or clawbacks (*takaisinperintä*).

## Why Reporting *Before* You Start Is Critical in Finland

In Finland, unemployment security is split between two authorities:
- **Labour policy assessment:** Your local municipal employment services (*työllisyyspalvelut* via JobMarket Finland, *Työmarkkinatori*) issue binding statements (*työvoimapoliittinen lausunto*) on whether you qualify as an unemployed jobseeker.
- **Benefit calculation and payment:** Either your unemployment fund (*työttömyyskassa*, for earnings-related allowance, *ansiopäiväraha*) or **Kela** (for basic allowance, *peruspäiväraha*, or labour market subsidy, *työmarkkinatuki*) calculates and pays your benefit.

If you are on unemployment benefit, **you must report self-employment before you start**. Here is why advance notification matters in Finland:

### 1. The Initial Four-Month Assessment Window
Under Finland's Unemployment Security Act (*Työttömyysturvalaki*), when you start a new self-employed activity while already unemployed, the employment authority generally does not assess whether the activity is full-time (*päätoiminen*) or part-time (*sivutoiminen*) during the first four months. During this initial four-month period, you typically remain eligible for adjusted unemployment benefits (*soviteltu työttömyysetuus*), provided you stay registered as a full-time jobseeker and report every euro earned.

### 2. Full-Time vs. Part-Time Entrepreneurship After Four Months
After four months, the employment authority evaluates the actual workload required by your contractor activity:
- **Part-time entrepreneurship (*sivutoiminen yrittäjyys*):** If the workload is small enough that you can still accept full-time employment immediately, your activity is deemed part-time and you can keep claiming adjusted benefits.
- **Full-time entrepreneurship (*päätoiminen yrittäjyys*):** If the workload prevents you from taking full-time employment, your entitlement to unemployment benefits ends—even if your actual monthly earnings are low or intermittent.

### 3. Avoiding Retroactive Clawbacks
If you log billable hours and receive Stripe payouts before notifying *Työmarkkinatori* and your benefit payer, your payments will be suspended pending an investigation, and previously paid benefits can be recovered (*takaisinperintä*). ## Managing Irregular Weekly USD Payouts During Adjustment Periods

If your employment authority confirms that you may claim adjusted unemployment benefits (*soviteltu työttömyysetuus*) while contracting part-time, you must reconcile **weekly USD payouts via Stripe** with **monthly or four-week euro benefit periods**. Although my NDA prevents me from sharing client-specific project details, these three administrative habits keep declarations accurate:

- **Log daily hours independently:** Record every date worked, exact minutes completed, gross USD earned, and the EUR amount credited to your bank account when Stripe settles.
- **Confirm document requirements with Kela or your fund:** Ask your benefit payer whether they require monthly Stripe payout summaries, bank conversion receipts, or a simple monthly income-and-expense statement (*selvitys yritystoiminnan tuloista*).
- **State clearly that hours are unguaranteed:** Inform your caseworker upfront that AI-training projects can pause at any time so zero-income weeks are properly understood.
