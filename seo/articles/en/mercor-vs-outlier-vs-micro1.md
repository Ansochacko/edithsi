## Deep Dive 2: How Outlier Works in Practice

Outlier is a large-scale AI-training platform that recruits both generalist contributors in regional European languages (including Nordic and Western European languages) and STEM/coding specialists.

### What Stands Out on Outlier:
- **Written Onboarding & Task-Queue Model:** Instead of starting with a conversational AI video interview on your résumé, Outlier frequently relies on written screening modules and unpaid or low-rate project-specific training courses before unlocking active task queues.
- **Location-Adjusted Rates:** Outlier often adjusts posted hourly rates based on your country of residence as well as your domain tier, and payouts in Europe commonly route through PayPal or AirTM rather than direct Stripe SEPA transfers.
- **Queue Volatility:** Even after you pass a project quiz, tasks are pulled from shared queues that can empty out mid-week without warning.

## Deep Dive 3: How micro1 Works in Practice

micro1 positions itself similarly to Mercor by using an automated AI interviewer to screen and certify technical and domain talent for AI labs and enterprise clients.

### What Stands Out on micro1:
- **AI Certification Badge Flow:** Applicants complete an automated video and technical screening session to enter micro1's certified talent pool.
- **Pool Waiting Times:** Just like Mercor, passing micro1's automated interview is a prerequisite for entering the candidate pool rather than a binding job offer. Many candidates pass the initial AI interview and wait weeks or months without matching to a paying client project.

## Why Many European Contractors Apply to More Than One Pool

If you ask experienced European independent contractors how they approach AI-training work, almost none of them rely on a single platform. Here is why diversifying across talent pools is the most rational strategy:

1. **Zero Exclusivity for Independent Contractors:** Because you are self-employed—not an employee—you are free to join multiple talent pools simultaneously, provided you never reuse confidential client prompts, never share data across platforms, and strictly honor every non-disclosure agreement (NDA) you sign.
2. **Project Cycles Are Unpredictable:** AI model training happens in sprints. A lab might fund a four-week sprint for Finnish or German language alignment, pause for three weeks to train checkpoints, and then launch a coding rubric sprint. Having active profiles in more than one pool reduces idle downtime between batches.
3. **Never Treat Any Pool as Guaranteed Income:** Even if you register on Mercor, Outlier, and micro1 simultaneously, there will be weeks where no platform has active tasks matching your profile. Treat all AI-training work as variable supplementary revenue.

## Tax, YEL, and Benefit Rules Apply Equally Across All Three Platforms

Regardless of whether your USD payouts arrive via Stripe from Mercor or via PayPal/Deel from another platform, European tax and social insurance authorities treat the income identically:

- **Finland Tax & YEL Example:** In Finland, all USD contractor payouts must be converted to euros (`EUR`) for bookkeeping. You must account for prepayment tax (*ennakkovero*), evaluate registering a sole proprietorship (*toiminimi*), and take out mandatory **YEL pension insurance** once your estimated annual work income reaches **`EUR 9,423/year` (2026)**. Read my full [guide to AI-training taxes, toiminimi, and YEL in Finland](/ai-training-taxes-finland-guide/) for details. (This is not tax advice.)
- **Unemployment Benefits:** If you receive unemployment benefits in Finland or elsewhere in Europe, you must report self-employment to the employment authorities **before** you start working on any platform.
